# The recap email

A recap is a short note that makes the meeting findable later and tells one
person what they agreed to do. It is not minutes and it is not a pitch.

## Shape

**Subject.** On a reply draft, leave the thread subject alone. On a new draft,
use the meeting name and the date: `Catch up, 14 October`.

**Body.**

1. `Hi [First name],` or `Hi all,` for three or more.
2. One line of thanks for their time. Warm, not effusive.
3. Two to four lines on what was covered. Plain past tense. What was discussed,
   what was decided, what was left open.
4. The actions, as a short list. Each one: who, what, and by when if a date was
   agreed. If no date was agreed, write "no date agreed yet" rather than
   inventing one.
5. One soft question. Usually a check that the recap matches what they heard, or
   the next step as a question.
6. `Thanks,` then the first name, then the signature block from `config.md`.

## Worked example, external

```
Hi Priya,

Thanks for your time this afternoon, it was good to talk it through properly.

To recap where we got to. You are running the pilot across two teams rather than
the whole department, starting after the November reporting cycle. The reporting
format is still open, and you wanted to see how the weekly view looks before
committing to it.

Actions from our side:

- I will send the weekly view example, by Friday.
- I will confirm who from our team joins the kickoff, by Friday.

And from yours, as I understood it:

- You will confirm the two teams, no date agreed yet.

Does that match what you had, at all? Happy to correct anything I have picked up
wrong.

Thanks,
Karl

Karl Wakley
Head of Product, Rethink Demand
rethinkdemand.com
```

## Worked example, internal

```
Hi both,

Quick note from this morning so we have it written down.

We agreed to hold the template change until after the sprint, and to take the
data question to Thursday's refinement rather than deciding it now.

- Sam: pull the current usage numbers before Thursday.
- Me: write the ticket.

Shout if I have got any of that wrong.

Thanks,
Karl
```

## What keeps it honest

- Only actions that were actually said. If the meeting produced no actions,
  write that it produced none. An invented action lands in someone's week.
- Only dates that were actually agreed. "By Friday" appears because someone said
  Friday.
- Attribute an action to a person only if that person took it in the meeting.
  Where it is unclear, put it under a line saying who picks it up is still open.
- No figures. No rates, volumes, budgets or deal values, even if they were the
  main subject. Refer to the topic and stop.
- No characterisation of anyone in the meeting. Record what was said.

## Voice check before finishing

Read the draft back and cut:

- Any em dash.
- Any sentence that ends in a comma plus an `-ing` clause commenting on the
  sentence.
- Any closing paragraph that repeats what the email already said.
- "Not only X but also Y", "more than just", "far from being".
- delve, pivotal, leverage, seamless, robust, crucial, comprehensive, showcase,
  notably, moreover, furthermore, deep dive, at its core, when it comes to.
- Any sentence that could be in any recap of any meeting. If it is not specific
  to this one, it is padding.
